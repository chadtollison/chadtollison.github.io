How I built and measured a PII redactor

scrubpii is my open-source tool for finding and redacting personal data on your own device. This page explains how I test it: which datasets, which configurations, how the scoring works and where the numbers can mislead. It compares against Microsoft Presidio, the most widely used open-source tool for the same job, run in its default setup.

10public test sets, in 5 languages
22,000+labeled personal-data items scored
90%of an item must be hidden to count
Publicdatasets, scoring code and results
The short version
  • The headline metric is strict. An item only counts as hidden if at least 90% of its characters are redacted, because half a phone number still leaks.
  • F1 is reported but not trusted alone. In this scorer it counts any overlap, so it can look perfect while data still leaks.
  • Better data beat a bigger model, and checksums fixed what the model couldn't be sure of.
  • It still has gaps, listed below, starting with unfamiliar financial paperwork.

02

How it's scored

The scorer ignores labels and asks one question: would this text be safe to share? If Presidio calls an address a LOCATION and scrubpii calls it an ADDRESS, both get full credit.

FULLY HIDDEN

Share of real personal-data items with at least 90% of their characters redacted. The headline number.

WAS PERSONAL

Share of finds that landed on real personal data. This catches a tool that redacts everything.

CAUGHT

Share of items that a find touches at all. Lenient: one character counts.

F1

Combines caught and was personal. Lenient for the same reason, so it's never the headline.

Overlap-based F1 doesn't guarantee redaction

My first charts led with F1. A review showed why that isn't enough: a deliberately broken detector that marks one character of each email gets a perfect F1 from this scorer, while fully hidden correctly shows zero. So every chart on this page uses fully hidden, and F1 only appears where I describe how a number changed during development.

Anyone can rerun it: the scoring code and the full results, with every metric are public.

03

Results

Share of personal data fully hidden, per test set. Read the non-English rows with the configuration note above in mind.

Precision matters too, or a tool could score well by redacting everything. On the English set, 99% of scrubpii's finds were real personal data, against 91% for Presidio's.

By type on the English set, the differences are mostly in things a pattern can't describe: names, street addresses, phone numbers written in unusual ways, and ID numbers. Emails are fully hidden by both.

04

What moved the numbers

I tracked every change against the hardest set: financial documents from a source the model never trained on. Long, dense and full of numbers that look personal but aren't. During development I tracked F1, so this chart uses it; on the strict score, the final version fully hides 78% of items here.

  1. Real data beat clever data

    Version 2 learned from sentences I generated. Version 3 trained on 500,000 realistic examples from ai4privacy (CC BY 4.0) and improved sharply on English. But the finance score dropped, from 0.70 to 0.65. That dip told me the next problem wasn't the data.

  2. Long documents needed a shorter window

    The model trained on short texts, then read long reports in 512-token chunks and lost track of what mattered. Reading in overlapping 128-token windows, the length it learned on, took finance from 0.65 to 0.76 without retraining.

  3. The model proposes, math confirms

    A model can guess that 16 digits are a card number. A Luhn checksum knows. I added validators for cards, SSNs, IBANs, IPs and real US area codes. They confirm or reject the model's finds and stretch a half-found item to its full length. Each find carries a reason, like model found it, rule confirmed: valid Luhn checksum, so a privacy reviewer can audit every redaction.

  4. The bigger model lost

    I tried DeBERTa-v3, a newer and larger model that usually wins on paper. It fully hid 52% of English personal data against 99% for the small DistilBERT model, and ran about 2.5 times slower. I kept the small one. Measuring before switching saved me from shipping a worse product that sounded better.

  5. Shipping small mattered as much as accuracy

    Nobody adds a 1.5 GB dependency to strip emails from logs. Exporting to ONNX with 8-bit weights removed PyTorch entirely, with no measurable change in accuracy.

  6. Broader data, then stop

    Version 5 added NVIDIA's Nemotron-PII set (medical notes, tickets, logs) plus French, German and Italian. Unseen finance rose to 0.845 F1 even though no finance data was added.

05

Limits and open gaps

Finance isn't solved

On unfamiliar financial paperwork, about one item in five isn't fully hidden. That's the next thing to fix.

SSNs and passports

Presidio fully hid every SSN and passport number in the English set; scrubpii hid 94% and 96%. Small groups, 18 and 24 items, but a miss is a miss.

Default configurations

Presidio is configurable: language models, custom recognizers, tuned thresholds. This compares defaults, which is what most people start with, not what a tuned deployment can reach.

Synthetic text

Most test sets are synthetic or semi-synthetic. Real documents are messier, which is why the never-seen finance set is the one I watch.
